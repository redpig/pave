# Principles

1. **Public-good infrastructure for local life.** Local work, local credit and local services that people own in common. Value stays with the participants because the fabric is cheap, open and transparent, not because someone promises to be fair.
2. **Values live in structure, not people.** Nothing to sell, nothing to capture and a credible exit for everyone. The rules are in the protocol, so a change of owner or operator can't change them.
3. **Open first.** An open spec, models and simulations come before any deployment, and anyone can implement them.
4. **A small, recursive core.** Three objects (scope, capability, message) and six invariants, short enough to fit on one page. Everything else, including the roles the fabric runs on, is built from them rather than added beside them.
5. **One kind of thing.** Money, credit, time, votes, certifications and access are all capabilities under a schema. A new use is a new schema, not a new feature.
6. **Data, not code.** Rules come from a closed menu of settings, and the kernel checks signatures and settings and never runs programs. Programmability would turn it into a weak blockchain.
7. **No shared clock, no conditionals.** Time is counted in issuers' own epochs, and the kernel keeps no time at all. There are no timelocks or conditional transfers, because they need agreed time and can be attacked when the internet is down but the local network is up. Evidence and proof of means replace locks.
8. **Local-first.** Everything works offline and merges later. Offline, misbehavior is detected rather than prevented, and the detection is reliable. Online services make things faster, never possible.
9. **The recipient decides.** An issuer chooses only what it mints and its operation rules. Who accepts what, and which roots to trust, is each recipient's choice, including whether to honor expiry. No issuer can tell anyone how to act.
10. **Nothing relies on a role behaving well.** What a role must guarantee is carried in what it presents and signs, so a role that misbehaves leaves evidence. Roles still keep latitude, on public terms.
11. **Nothing acts as you.** No kernel, role or service can sign, recover or reissue on a holder's behalf. Recovery is something each holder sets up for itself.
12. **Many kernels, joined by choice.** Anyone can run a kernel, and a town can have many. They connect only where participants choose to accept each other's credit or credentials. Nothing syncs between them by decree.
13. **Unlinkable by default; only misbehavior links you.** Each use reveals only the rights it carries. Using the same thing twice is what links two uses, and there is no master unmasking key.
14. **Linkage is the holder's choice.** A stable identity is something a holder presents, to build a relationship or to accept regulated work, not something the system imposes. Disclosure goes step by step: unlinked proofs to reply, and identity only where the job requires it.
15. **Transparency of rules, privacy of people.** Every scope's rules, every mint and every transfer's public summary are open to all. Personal data stays off the ledger, and receipts are revealed branch by branch, only by their parties.
16. **Incentives over enforcement.** Where money is involved, enforcement gets circumvented. So make abuse unprofitable by design and keep detection as the backstop.
17. **Consent-gated reach.** A message reaches you only under a capability you granted, or by spending something. Rate limits are per person, not per account or agent.
18. **Lower assurance means tighter limits, not exclusion.** People without strong identity documents can still take part, with smaller limits.
19. **Not an MLM.** Rewards come only from real external revenue: no multi-level referral payouts, no buy-in to earn, and any early-adopter upside is bounded and comes from a closed cohort.
20. **The work of running the fabric is work on the fabric.** Hosting, relays, moderation, mediation, audits and escrow are posted as jobs and paid like any other. Every check ends in something anyone can verify, and there is no fee on a fee.
21. **Invisible to users.** No one needs to understand credentials, keys, tickets, epochs or proofs. People make only human decisions; wallets handle the protocol.
22. **Split in the protocol, merged in operation.** One operator can run every role at first, each under its own key, and hand roles to others later without users noticing. Non-money uses such as jobs, messages and reputation give people reasons to sync, beyond pressure from payments.
23. **Prove it before locking it.** No part of the core is called final until models show safety under adversarial members and partitions, and simulations show the incentives hold at scale.
24. **Test credit only until there is an accountable operator.** Anything redeemable for money waits for one.
